## One-line summary
A training framework combining temporal-token embedding augmentation (TIAE) and contrastive reinforcement learning with granular negatives (GCRL) improves LLM accuracy on time-sensitive QA by up to 30% EM over supervised fine-tuning baselines.

## What they did
Addressed two failure modes of LLMs on time-sensitive QA: (1) insufficient attention to temporal tokens in input, and (2) weak temporal reasoning — susceptibility to answers from wrong time periods or co-occurring events. Method: Temporal Information-Aware Embedding (TIAE) uses SpaCy NER to locate temporal expressions in question and context, builds binary attention matrices, extends marked positions with a sliding window (size 10), and adds a learned temporal embedding on top of the text embedding. Granular Contrastive Reinforcement Learning (GCRL) constructs two categories of hard negatives: remote negatives (same entity/relation, different time period) and proximal negatives (same time period, different entity/relation). Uses triplet loss as reward function in PPO rather than exact-match. Evaluated on TempReason L2/L3 and TimeQA Easy/Hard.

## Key findings
- TIAE alone: +23.6% EM on TempReason-L2 OBQA over T5-SFT baseline.
- GCRL alone: +29.7% EM on TempReason-L2 OBQA over T5-SFT baseline.
- Combined: +30.4% EM (diminishing returns, but still additive).
- Remote and proximal negatives contribute roughly equally (~28–29% individually); using both gives +30%.
- On structured context (ReasonQA), gains are even larger: +12–20% EM over prior SOTA (TempT5).
- EM reward function is inferior to triplet contrastive reward (fixes semantic-equivalent mismatches like "capital of France" vs "Paris").
- LLMs without special handling score ~8–17% EM on OBQA temporal QA (ChatGPT included); human-level performance remains far above current models.

## Relevance to our experiment
How this connects to: "attempt count (not elapsed time) reduces agent tool-use turns in debugging tasks"

Most directly relevant to the question of *what kind of temporal signal matters*. TSQA distinguishes two temporal failure modes: sensitivity (attending to the time token) and reasoning (correctly placing an answer within the right temporal interval vs. adjacent ones). The remote/proximal negative taxonomy directly mirrors our question: is the agent confused by *temporally adjacent* attempts (proximal error — wrong attempt but same time period) or *semantically similar* past states (remote error — right type of action but wrong episode)? If attempt count reduces tool-use turns, GCRL's framework suggests the mechanism: the agent learns to suppress answers from "the wrong attempt" (remote negatives in our terminology), not just semantically wrong ones. TIAE's approach of marking temporal tokens is analogous to marking attempt-count tokens explicitly in agent context.

## Gaps / what they didn't do
- Entirely focused on reading comprehension QA, not on agentic tool-use or iterative task loops.
- Temporal signal is always an explicit date/year/event label in the text — not an implicit count of prior attempts.
- Does not examine whether training on temporal discrimination changes *how many steps* an agent takes, only whether it gets the answer right.
- Evaluation metric is EM/F1, not turn-count efficiency or task-completion rate.
- No study of whether ordinal position (attempt 3 of 5) vs. absolute time (year 2009) is the operative cue.
- Method is supervised/RL training, not in-context; findings may not transfer to inference-time temporal awareness.

## Key quotes
> "LLMs significantly lack attention to and comprehension of temporal information in both questions and their contexts... It is essential for models to prioritize temporal cues in questions to accurately locate corresponding times and facts within contexts."

> "Remote Negative Answers are those that share the same subject and relation with the fact but belong to distinctly different time periods... Proximal Negative Answers... are selected from different subjects or relations but fall within the same time period as the ground truth."

> "Modifying the temporal information within the question will result in a change to the answer." [Definition of TSQA's core property]

> "LLMs exhibit notably weak temporal reasoning abilities. This specifically refers to the models' susceptibility to interference from answers that pertain to the same entity or relationship but span different time periods."
