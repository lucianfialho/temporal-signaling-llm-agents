## One-line summary
Injecting an explicit token budget into chain-of-thought prompts reduces reasoning token usage by 68% while maintaining accuracy within 5%.

## What they did
TALE (Token-Budget-Aware LLM rEasoning) adds a token constraint to CoT prompts ("think step by step and use less than N tokens"). The key finding is "token elasticity": an optimal budget exists — too small and the model ignores it and uses *more* tokens; reasonable budgets produce efficient, correct reasoning. They use binary search to find the optimal budget per problem and model.

## Key findings
- Including a token budget in CoT prompts reduces output tokens by 68% on average.
- Accuracy drops less than 5% across math and reasoning benchmarks.
- "Token elasticity": unreasonably small budgets cause token usage to *increase*, not decrease.
- Effect works across GPT-4o-mini, Yi-lightning, and other models.
- The optimal budget is problem-specific and must be estimated, not fixed globally.

## Relevance to our experiment
TALE is the closest analogue in the budget-prompting literature. Both TALE and our work show that resource constraint signals in prompts change LLM planning behavior. The key distinction:

- **TALE**: token count budget → constrains reasoning verbosity in *single inference calls*
- **Our work**: attempt count budget → constrains action selection in *multi-step agentic loops* with tool use and external test oracles

TALE does not study: elapsed time, temporal signals, agentic tool-use loops, multi-model capability effects, or pass/fail task oracles. Our finding that effect direction reverses between Sonnet and Opus has no analogue in TALE.

TALE supports the broader claim that "budget signals work" and is the most relevant prior work to cite defensively.

## Gaps / what they didn't do
- Single-call reasoning only — no tool use, no agent loops.
- No temporal signals (elapsed time, session duration).
- No multi-model capability comparison.
- No agentic tasks with external feedback.
- Budget is on output tokens, not on action count or iterations.
